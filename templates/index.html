Overview

What the theory predicts

Drop impact on a rigid surface depends on the drop velocity, size and material properties. Two dimensionless groups organise the scaling theory: the Weber number \(We\), which compares inertia with surface tension, and the Ohnesorge number \(Oh\), which measures viscous effects.

The theory is built from direct numerical simulations spanning \(1 \leq We \leq 10^3\) and \(10^{-3} \leq Oh \leq 10^2\). It separates the dominant dissipation mechanisms across the impact process and predicts the maximum spreading diameter, showing why viscous dissipation remains important even at low \(Oh\).

Inputs

Dimensionless groups

Weber number

\(We = \dfrac{\rho V_0^2 D_0}{\gamma}\)

Inertia relative to surface tension. Larger values mean stronger inertial forcing of the spreading lamella.

Ohnesorge number

\(Oh = \dfrac{\mu}{\sqrt{\rho \gamma D_0}}\)

Viscous relative to inertio-capillary effects. Larger values mean stronger viscous damping during impact.
